<div class="block">
<p>My research follows families through major life events, like a dementia diagnosis or a spell of homelessness, and traces what happens to their work and income. I combine large administrative datasets from Colombia and the United States with causal inference methods, including difference-in-differences, matching, and discrete-choice models, to produce evidence that can guide policy decisions.</p>
</div>

<div class="block">
<p class="label">Job market paper</p>
<p class="paper-title"><a href="/research/">The Economic Consequences of Alzheimer's and Dementia: Evidence from Patients and Households in Colombia</a></p>
<p class="meta">with Christian Posso and Andrea Franco Hernandez</p>
<p class="summary">Two years after a first dementia diagnosis, patients' formal employment is about 15 percentage points lower, and other household members' formal employment and earnings fall as well. The economic burden of dementia reaches the whole household, not just the person diagnosed.</p>
