<div class="block">
<p>My research follows families through major life events, like a dementia diagnosis or a spell of homelessness, and traces what happens to their work and income. I combine large administrative datasets from Colombia and the United States with causal inference methods, including difference-in-differences, matching, and discrete-choice models, to produce evidence that can guide policy decisions.</p>
</div>

<div class="block">
<p class="label">Job market paper</p>
<p class="paper-title"><a href="/research/">The Labor-Market Costs of Dementia for Patients and Families: Evidence from Colombia</a></p>
<p class="meta">with Andrea Franco Hernandez and Christian Posso</p>
<p class="summary">Two years after a first dementia diagnosis, patients' formal employment is about 10 percentage points lower for women and 15 points lower for men. The share of other household members with formal jobs is about 5 percentage points lower, and their formal earnings fall as well, for women and men alike. In the formal labor market, the costs of dementia extend from patients to their families.</p>
