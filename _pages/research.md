## Job Market Paper

### The Economic Consequences of Alzheimer's and Dementia: Evidence from Patients and Households in Colombia
with Christian Posso and Andrea Franco Hernandez

Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD) can reduce a patient's ability to work while increasing care needs at home. We study how formal employment and earnings change for patients and their household members in Colombia, where public long-term care is limited and multigenerational living is common. Linking national health records to monthly formal-employment data and SISBEN household rosters, we estimate stacked difference-in-differences event studies comparing newly diagnosed patients and their households with matched, never-diagnosed comparisons. Two years after a first recorded diagnosis, working-age patients' formal employment is about 15 percentage points lower. Among other working-age household members, formal employment is 3.5 percentage points lower after one year and 4.5 points lower after two, and earnings decline for both women and men, providing no evidence that relatives offset the patient's losses through their own formal work. These results document household-level losses in formal-sector attachment that patient-level analyses miss.

## Working Papers

### [Homelessness and the Persistence of Deprivation: Income, Employment, and Safety Net Participation](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32323)
with Bruce D. Meyer, Angela Wyse, Gillian Meyer, and Derek Wu. NBER Working Paper 32323, 2024.

Homelessness is arguably the most extreme hardship associated with poverty in the United States, yet people experiencing homelessness are excluded from official poverty statistics. Starting from a large, nationally representative sample of sheltered and unsheltered homeless adults in the 2010 Census, we link longitudinal tax records and administrative data on SNAP, Medicare, Medicaid, DI, SSI, veterans' benefits, housing assistance, and mortality to provide the first national administrative estimates of this population's income, employment, and safety net participation. Nearly half had formal employment in the year they were observed as homeless, and nearly all either worked or participated in at least one safety net program. Yet their incomes remained low throughout the decade surrounding homelessness, suggesting that homelessness arises from long-term, severe deprivation rather than sudden income losses.

### State Income Taxes and Retiree Migration: Evidence from Household-Specific Tax Incentives

States compete for retirees through tax preferences for Social Security, pensions, and other retirement income, but it is unclear whether these policies induce retirees to move or only shape where movers settle. Using CPS ASEC data from 1982–2019 and TAXSIM, I calculate the after-tax income each retiree household would receive in every state given its own mix of income, and estimate conditional logit models of location choice. Among retirees overall, after-tax income has little effect on where they live, and distance from the origin state is a much stronger predictor. Among interstate movers, however, retirees are significantly more likely to choose states where they would receive higher after-tax income. State tax policy appears to do little to induce retirees to relocate, but it can shape competition for those who have already decided to move.

### Household Criminality and the Intergenerational Transmission of Disadvantage: Evidence from Medellín, Colombia
with Christian Posso

This paper examines how children's outcomes change when an adult who becomes involved in crime enters their household. Using linked household survey, education, and crime records from Medellín, we compare children who experience the arrival of such an adult with children who experience the arrival of an adult who does not commit a crime. Children exposed to a criminally involved adult are 9–10 percentage points more likely to drop out of school and 1–1.3 percentage points more likely to have a recorded criminal offense, and those who take the high-school mathematics exam score 0.05–0.06 standard deviations lower. The estimates are similar after matching on child, household, and mover characteristics.
